Berkeley and Flock: what the record shows

Twenty-five points, each drawn from a verified source and linked to the document it came from. The decision returns to Council in 2027.

The decision

What Council did, and what is still open.

  1. 01
    Council rejected the expansion 8–1.

    On May 7, 2026 Council voted not to execute Flock's full Master Services Agreement — fixed cameras, community video streams, first-responder drones, investigations software. City Clerk — May 7, 2026

  2. 02
    The extension passed 5–4.

    A separate, severed vote extended the existing 52-camera contract for twelve months at up to $200,000. Four members voted no: Bartlett, Tregub, Lunaparra, Ishii. City Clerk — May 7, 2026

  3. 03
    The successor was sent to an RFP.

    Council referred procurement of each MSA component to the City Manager, in consultation with the Police Accountability Board and BPD. City Clerk — May 7, 2026

  4. 04
    A review is required before the extension ends.

    An adopted amendment requires an efficacy assessment and compliance review, presented to Council at least 30 days before expiry. City Clerk — May 7, 2026

  5. 05
    That lands around June 2027.

    The extension runs roughly twelve months from July 2026. The Council receiving the review is the one seated December 1, 2026 — not the one that ordered it. City Clerk — May 7, 2026

  6. 06
    Staff control when it comes back.

    Under BMC 2.99.030 the City Manager decides whether and when a surveillance acquisition report reaches an agenda. City Clerk — May 7, 2026

What Berkeley got right

Stated first, because it is the strongest part of the City's record.

  1. 07
    Berkeley has banned a surveillance capability before.

    In 2019 Council passed to print an ordinance amending the same municipal code chapter to prohibit City use of face recognition technology. Berkeley — Oct 15, 2019

  2. 08
    Council imposed real restrictions, 8–1.

    July 2025 directives barred sharing with federal immigration enforcement and with out-of-state agencies enforcing laws against reproductive or gender-affirming care, and required biannual audits. City Clerk — Jul 22, 2025

  3. 09
    BPD cut off out-of-region access.

    By order of the Chief, access was revoked for every agency outside the nine Bay Area counties plus Sacramento, and the statewide lookup tool was turned off. Office of the City Manager — Oct 22, 2025

  4. 10
    BPD found the problem itself.

    In July 2025 the department identified noncompliant use through the statewide lookup function and disabled the feature. Berkeley Police Accountability Board — Jul 2025

Where the rules stop

Two failures in the past year that compliance rules did not reach.

  1. 11
    The audit measures rule-following, not data movement.

    It reports 100 percent compliance on three measures and 99.81 percent on external permitted uses — that is, whether authorized users searched for permitted reasons. Office of the City Manager — Oct 22, 2025

  2. 12
    Mountain View: the vendor changed the setting.

    Berkeley's Police Accountability Board reports Flock silently enabled nationwide access there, with roughly 600,000 unauthorized searches over more than a year. That council terminated its contract unanimously. Police Accountability Board — Feb 24, 2026

  3. 13
    San Francisco: the failure was at the second hop.

    KQED reports SFPD lawfully authorized NCRIC, which extended access to WSIN analysts who searched for outside agencies. Both were disabled. KQED — Jun 18, 2026

  4. 14
    It took a year and an audit to surface.

    The San Francisco Standard reported 299 improper inquiries over roughly a year — about 0.005 percent of queries — found by routine compliance audit. The San Francisco Standard — Jun 18, 2026

  5. 15
    Berkeley has not named the agencies involved.

    At the November 5, 2025 Police Accountability Board meeting, BPD declined to identify them, citing investigatory records. Berkeley Police Accountability Board — Nov 5, 2025

Does it work

The City's figures, and the best published study.

  1. 16
    The City reports 55 arrests in 2025.

    Also more than 100 other investigations assisted, at least 43 stolen vehicles recovered, and robbery closure rates up ten points over nine months. Self-reported, with no comparison group. Berkeley — Jan 20, 2026

  2. 17
    The strongest study is mixed.

    A Justice Evaluation Journal evaluation of Atlantic City found the ALPR expansion did not reduce violent crime, but was associated with reductions in shootings, vehicle thefts, and property crime. Justice Evaluation Journal — Mar 1, 2025

  3. 18
    That city is a barrier island.

    The cameras covered all entrances and exits, scanning every vehicle in and out. Berkeley has no comparable perimeter. Justice Evaluation Journal — Jul 3, 2025

  4. 19
    Its co-author runs the department studied.

    James A. Sarkos, Chief of the Atlantic City Police Department, is one of the two authors of the evaluation of his own agency's program. Atlantic City — Jul 1, 2022

What it costs

From the City's own budget documents.

  1. 20
    The original contract was $344,500.

    Authorized on the Consent Calendar in October 2023, with a ceiling not to exceed $425,000. City Clerk — Oct 10, 2023

  2. 21
    It was never pulled for discussion.

    At least 17 written communications were logged on the item. It was disposed of on the Consent Calendar. City Clerk — Oct 10, 2023

  3. 22
    Three sworn positions were to be cut to pay for it.

    Item 26 proposed reducing authorized sworn strength by three full-time positions, with the salary savings funding technology subscriptions. Office of the City Manager — Mar 24, 2026

  4. 23
    The 10 percent discount was not on the renewal.

    The staff report states it applied to new product lines — drones, PTZ cameras, Nova — if contracts executed by end of March 2026. The ALPR renewal carried the existing rate. Office of the City Manager — Mar 24, 2026

  5. 24
    Staff presented the deadline as a reason to hurry.

    The report describes the expiring discount as providing a compelling fiscal incentive to act now. Office of the City Manager — Mar 24, 2026
